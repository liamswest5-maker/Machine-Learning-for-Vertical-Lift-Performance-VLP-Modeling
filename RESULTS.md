## Validation Results

### Strategy A: Leave-One-Well-Out (LOWO)

*The strictest test — can the model predict a completely unseen well?*

| Held-Out Well | n_test | n_train | RMSE (bar) | MAE (bar) | MAPE (%) | R² |
|:-------------|:------:|:-------:|:----------:|:---------:|:--------:|:--:|
| F-1C | 390 | 5,240 | 15.48 | 14.37 | 7.96 | 0.447 |
| F-11H | 1,063 | 4,567 | 7.76 | 5.95 | 3.24 | **0.862** |
| F-12H | 894 | 4,736 | 18.00 | 16.27 | 8.52 | −0.023 |
| F-14H | 2,553 | 3,077 | 15.42 | 12.43 | 6.62 | 0.663 |
| F-15D | 730 | 4,900 | 18.25 | 16.69 | 10.19 | −1.099 |
| **Mean** | — | — | **14.98** | **13.14** | **7.30** | **0.170** |
| **Std** | — | — | 4.26 | — | — | 0.77 |

**Key observations:**
- **F-11H** generalizes best (R²=0.86) — it has the most data and diverse operating conditions
- **F-15D** performs worst (R²=−1.10) — it operates in a narrow ΔP range (~150–170 bar) not seen in training data
- **F-12H** is near-zero R² — similar issue with unique operating regime
- The mean R² of 0.17 is honest: cross-well prediction is challenging with only 5 wells

> **Data file:** `data/lowo_results.csv`

### Strategy B: Chronological Within-Well Split

*Train on first 80% of each well's timeline + all other wells. Test on last 20%.*

|:-----|:------:|:-------:|:----------:|:---------:|:--------:|:--:|
| F-1C | 78 | 5,552 | 9.25 | 7.75 | 3.79 | 0.547 |

| F-12H | 179 | 5,451 | 21.00 | 20.06 | 9.20 | −5.624 |
| F-14H | 511 | 5,119 | 5.10 | 4.83 | 2.03 | −6.644 |
| F-15D | 146 | 5,484 | 7.58 | 6.54 | 3.54 | −0.046 |
| **Mean** | — | — | **9.86** | **8.78** | **4.14** | **−2.28** |

**Key observations:**
- Lower RMSE than LOWO on average (9.86 vs 14.98) — having partial well history helps
- Negative R² for F-12H and F-14H indicates the test period (last 20%) has a fundamentally different operating regime than the training period (first 80%), e.g., rising water cut late in well life
- **Best practical use case:** wells where early production data is available

> **Data file:** `data/chrono_split_results.csv`

| Pooled random | **4.12** | **0.74** | **0.976** | Excellent — interpolation works very well |

The gap between pooled (R²=0.976) and LOWO (R²=0.170) reveals that **well-specific patterns**
contribute significantly to predictions. The model is excellent at interpolating within known
conditions but struggles to extrapolate to fundamentally different wells.

### Key Insights

1. **Compositional features dominate:** GLR + WC + q_wat = **77.9%** of total importance
2. **GLR > WC** — the gas-liquid ratio is a more direct indicator of multiphase flow regime than water cut alone
3. **Temperature matters:** Downhole temperature + temperature gradient = 9.5%
4. **Pressure (AVG_WHP_P) has low individual importance** — this makes physical sense because ΔP is computed as P_wf − P_wh, so WHP is embedded in the target
5. **ON_STREAM_HRS** is nearly irrelevant (0.1%) — after normalizing rates by hours, the raw hours add no predictive value

## RF vs Beggs & Brill Benchmark

⚠️ **Caveat:** The Beggs & Brill correlation uses **assumed** tubing geometry (ID=4.892 in,
depth=3100 m, inclination=65°). These are representative values, not measured from well
completion reports. The RF model does not use these assumptions.

### Per-Well Comparison

| Well | RF RMSE (bar) | B&B RMSE (bar) | RF Improvement | RF R² | B&B R² |
|------|:------------:|:--------------:|:--------------:|:-----:|:------:|
| F-1C | 15.48 | 67.94 | **77%** | 0.447 | −9.66 |
| F-11H | 7.76 | 61.87 | **87%** | 0.862 | −7.80 |
| F-12H | 18.00 | 53.88 | **67%** | −0.023 | −8.17 |
| F-14H | 15.42 | 64.53 | **76%** | 0.663 | −4.90 |
| F-15D | 18.25 | 69.11 | **74%** | −1.099 | −29.09 |

### Key Observations

1. **RF outperforms B&B on every well** — even the worst RF fold (F-15D, RMSE=18.25) is far better than B&B (RMSE=69.11)
2. **B&B consistently overpredicts** — likely because assumed geometry is not representative
3. **B&B R² is deeply negative for all wells** — the correlation produces worse predictions than simply guessing the mean ΔP
4. With **real well geometry**, B&B performance would likely improve, but the RF advantage would likely persist

> **Data files:** `data/beggs_brill_results.csv`, `data/rf_vs_bb_comparison.csv`
>
> **Figure:** `figures/fig5_rf_vs_bb.png`

## Interpretation & Discussion

### What the Model Does Well

1. **Interpolation** — When test data comes from the same well distribution as training data (pooled split), the model achieves R²=0.976
2. **Best-case generalization** — For F-11H, which has diverse conditions well-represented across other wells, LOWO R²=0.862
3. **Outperforms B&B** — Even with assumed geometry disadvantaging B&B, the RF advantage is overwhelming (67–87% lower RMSE)

### Where the Model Struggles

1. **Narrow ΔP range wells** — F-15D operates in a ~150–170 bar range with little variation; the model trained on other wells (which span 50–300 bar) struggles to resolve this narrow band
2. **Unique operating regimes** — F-12H has characteristics not represented in the other 4 wells
3. **Temporal regime shifts** — When a well's late-life behavior (rising WC, declining rates) differs fundamentally from its early life, chronological prediction suffers

### Practical Recommendations

1. **Best use case:** Predicting ΔP for a well where you have **some** historical data (chronological scenario)
2. **For new wells:** LOWO results suggest the model needs wells with similar operating conditions in the training set
3. **Combine with domain knowledge:** Use the feature importance results to validate that the model is learning physically meaningful patterns (it is — compositional features dominate)
